Entropy and cross-entropy: how surprised are we?

Draw one ball from the bowl. A colour with probability p surprises us by −log p. Entropy is the average surprise under the true bowl p; cross-entropy measures the surprise with a model q while the balls still come from p.

True bowl p: entropy

H(p) = −∑i pi log pi

Model q: cross-entropy

H(p, q) = −∑i pi log qi

Moving one slider rescales the other colours of the same bowl, so the probabilities always sum to 1. The bowls show 16 balls, rounded to the nearest whole ball.

Draw balls from the true bowl

Each ball costs −log p(colour) with the true bowl and −log q(colour) with the model. Their running averages converge to H(p) and H(p, q): cross-entropy is just the average loss of the model on data from p.
